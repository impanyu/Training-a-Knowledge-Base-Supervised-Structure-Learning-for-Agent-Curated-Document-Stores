\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation treats the document store as a frozen input. A recent
wave of systems instead lets an LLM agent continuously curate a persistent knowledge
base, yet none of this work measures what curation does to the store itself. We invert
the usual framing: \emph{the knowledge base is the model}. A training agent consumes
supervised QA pairs one at a time---attempting each question against the current store,
receiving the gold answer, then consolidating verified reasoning into the store's
structure---and an independent frozen-store reader is examined on held-out questions
under a fixed action budget. Our central hypothesis is an \emph{efficiency} claim:
by materializing reasoning paths into index and shortcut structure, a trained store
lets an unchanged reader answer at \emph{non-inferior accuracy in fewer steps},
amortizing one-time training cost across all future queries. The protocol yields the
full apparatus of machine learning for knowledge bases: learning curves, a train/test
generalization gap, per-class transfer, and a break-even analysis of training cost
against per-question savings. On a contamination-free synthetic universe with
document-level ground truth, our first controlled result is cautionary: on an
already-organized store, additive curation without a parsimony objective is net
harmful---the agent quintupled the store and \emph{regressed} reader accuracy by 17
F1 points---motivating the explicit parsimony objective in our final protocol.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The dominant way to give language models durable knowledge is retrieval-augmented
generation (RAG): embed a corpus once, retrieve at query time, never write back. A
rapidly growing line of work breaks this asymmetry. Agent-maintained wikis compile
sources into evolving page networks~\cite{llmwiki-rar,llmwiki-stream,karpathy-llmwiki};
agentic memory systems accumulate and link notes across sessions~\cite{amem}; and
``sleep-time'' agents reorganize memory between conversations~\cite{letta-sleep}. The
pattern has reached practice ahead of measurement: the community's own description of
the wiki pattern lists a \emph{lint} step---checking contradictions, stale claims,
orphan pages, missing cross-links---as an engineering habit with no formal evaluation
attached~\cite{karpathy-llmwiki}, and the closest published systems evaluate only
downstream answer accuracy, never the store~\cite{llmwiki-rar,llmwiki-stream}.

We study the store directly, under the oldest framing machine learning has:
\emph{train/test}. The knowledge base is the model. Supervised questions arrive one at
a time; a training agent answers each from the current store, is graded, and then, in
an explicit consolidation phase, edits the store's structure so that the question's
\emph{class} becomes easier for a future reader. Evaluation freezes the store and
examines an independent reader with no gold access and a fixed action budget on
held-out questions.

The value produced by such training is best understood as \emph{index construction}:
a database does not change its data when an index is built, yet queries get cheaper.
Each training question's verified reasoning path is materialized into structure---a
multi-hop chain becomes a traversable link path, a scattered family of facts becomes a
navigation node---so future readers pay a lookup price instead of a re-derivation
price. Training cost is thereby amortized: after a computable number of questions, the
one-time cost of curation is repaid by per-question savings.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Agent-trained knowledge bases.} We propose letting an LLM agent
\emph{train} a document store under the standard machine-learning training
paradigm, with the knowledge base playing the role of the model: supervised
(question, answer) pairs are consumed one at a time over multiple epochs; each
iteration runs a forward pass (answer the question from the current store) and
a backward pass (with the revealed gold in hand, \emph{edit the store
itself}---adding, rewriting, deleting, and linking documents; building index
and navigation documents---so the question's class becomes easier for a future
reader); held-out performance is the loss. The knowledge base, not the model
weights, is the learned object; its structure is carved incrementally by the
questions actually asked, unlike offline graph construction built blind to
the query distribution.
\item \textbf{A train/test protocol for knowledge bases}: two-phase
(forward/backward) training iterations, single-shot gold reveal,
frozen-store examination under action budgets, learning curves, and a
three-layer generalization decomposition (re-encountered instances / unseen
instances / unseen templates).
\item \textbf{\kbgym}, a contamination-free environment in the PhantomWiki
style~\cite{phantomwiki}: a fictional-person universe rendered as a graph of
atomic single-sentence documents; ten diagnostic question classes with exact
answers and support sets; deterministic, integer-exact store metrics; no LLM
judge anywhere.
\item \textbf{Findings with practical teeth}: a controlled negative
result---on a pre-organized store, agent curation without a parsimony
objective monotonically pollutes (duplicated facts bury each other in
retrieval)---and cost as a first-class outcome: accuracy, store size,
training cost, and per-question answering cost reported together with a
break-even analysis of when training a store pays for itself.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Generation}
Classic RAG retrieves from a frozen corpus with a learned dense retriever and
generates conditioned on the results~\cite{rag-lewis,dpr,realm,fid}. The
corpus is an input, never an output: nothing the system learns at answer time
flows back into the store. Our work targets exactly this missing backward
edge.

\subsection{Agent-Maintained Knowledge Bases}
Self-evolving LLM-wiki retrieval~\cite{llmwiki-rar} has a single agent write and
update wiki pages during question answering, outperforming static RAG on multi-hop
benchmarks; streaming knowledge compilation~\cite{llmwiki-stream} maintains a
time-evolving wiki under a document stream. STORM~\cite{storm} generates
Wikipedia-style articles with multi-perspective research agents---one-shot
authorship rather than continual maintenance. The published systems evaluate
downstream task scores only:
the wiki itself is never measured, there is no train/test split, and the evaluation
corpora (HotpotQA, MuSiQue) are parametrically contaminated. Our protocol adds the
missing measurement layer.

\subsection{Agentic Retrieval}
Agentic RAG moves retrieval into the reasoning loop: ReAct interleaves
reasoning with tool calls~\cite{react}, IRCoT interleaves chain-of-thought
with retrieval~\cite{ircot}, Self-Ask decomposes into sub-questions before
searching~\cite{selfask}, FLARE retrieves when generation confidence
drops~\cite{flare}, Self-RAG learns when to retrieve and how to judge what
returns~\cite{selfrag}, and recent systems frame the retrieve-or-reason
decision as an MDP (DeepRAG~\cite{deeprag}) or train the search policy with
reinforcement learning (Search-R1~\cite{searchr1}; surveyed
in~\cite{agentic-rag-survey}). Our reader \emph{is} an agentic-RAG loop---budgeted
iterative search-and-read. The distinction is which side of the loop learns: that line
improves the \emph{query-side policy} against a fixed corpus; we hold the policy fixed
and train the \emph{store}. The axes are orthogonal and composable; our budget sweep
measures exactly what a better store buys an unchanged agentic reader.

\subsection{Offline Structure Construction}
GraphRAG~\cite{graphrag}, RAPTOR~\cite{raptor}, LightRAG~\cite{lightrag}, and
HippoRAG~\cite{hipporag,hipporag2} build entity graphs, summary hierarchies,
or PageRank-linked knowledge graphs over a corpus \emph{before} any question
arrives. These are our strongest baselines and the natural
contrast: their structure is unsupervised and built blind to the query distribution;
ours is carved incrementally by the questions actually asked.

\subsection{Agent Memory and Experience Accumulation}
A rich line lets agents accumulate experience across episodes: MemGPT pages
context in and out of external memory~\cite{memgpt}; Mem0 and A-MEM maintain
linked long-term note stores~\cite{mem0,amem}; generative agents retrieve and
reflect over an event stream~\cite{generative-agents}; Voyager grows a skill
library~\cite{voyager}; Reflexion, ExpeL, and Agent Workflow Memory distill
verbal feedback, cross-task insights, and reusable workflows,
respectively~\cite{reflexion,expel,awm}; Dynamic Cheatsheet and ACE evolve a
test-time strategy memory or context playbook~\cite{cheatsheet,ace}. In all
of these the artifact is a prompt or a private memory serving one agent; ours
is a shared, inspectable document store whose content is
ground-truth-checkable and whose curation quality is itself the measured
outcome.

\subsection{Memory Evaluation and Contamination Control}
LongMemEval and successors probe assistant memory with QA over past sessions,
typically via LLM judges~\cite{longmemeval}; LoCoMo evaluates very-long-term
conversational memory~\cite{locomo}; MemDelta documents hidden confounds and
argues for controlled baselines~\cite{memdelta}, supporting our no-store
baseline discipline. Multi-hop QA datasets---HotpotQA~\cite{hotpotqa},
MuSiQue~\cite{musique}, 2WikiMultiHopQA~\cite{twowiki}---supply real-text
questions with annotated support, but their public corpora are parametrically
contaminated for current models. PhantomWiki~\cite{phantomwiki} generates
contamination-free fictional universes with programmatically exact answers;
our generator follows its recipe, and SQuAD-style normalization~\cite{squad}
provides the grading.

\subsection{Knowledge Editing}
ROME and MEMIT edit factual associations \emph{inside model
weights}~\cite{rome,memit}. We pursue the non-parametric complement: facts
live in an external store the agent can inspect, reorganize, and be audited
on---no gradient access, full provenance, and edits that transfer across
models. STaR-style bootstrapping~\cite{star} keeps only verified reasoning
for further training; our forward/backward iteration inherits that
predict-then-learn shape, with the store rather than the weights as the
learned object.

\section{Problem Formulation}
A knowledge base $K$ is a directed graph of \emph{documents}: each document holds
one self-contained sentence and a set of outgoing links. The \emph{reader} is a fixed
LLM policy with three tools---vector \texttt{search}, node \texttt{read} (text plus
linked nodes), and \texttt{answer}---a fixed action budget $M$, and a short-term
memory holding only the current question and the last $k$ action--result pairs.
Reader performance on a question distribution $Q$ defines store quality along two
axes:
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{acc}(K) &= \mathbb{E}_{q\sim Q}\!\left[\mathrm{F1}\big(\mathrm{reader}(K,q)\big)\right],\\
\mathrm{cost}(K) &= \mathbb{E}_{q\sim Q}\!\left[\mathrm{steps}\big(\mathrm{reader}(K,q)\big)\right].
\end{align*}
\emph{Training} is any procedure that consumes supervised pairs $(q, a^{*})$ and edits
$K$, producing $K_T$ from the initial store $K_0$. Our central hypothesis is a joint
condition on the two axes:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{acc}(K_T) \;\ge\; \mathrm{acc}(K_0) - \delta
\quad\text{and}\quad
\rho \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{cost}(K_T)}{\mathrm{cost}(K_0)} \;<\; 1,
\label{eq:hypothesis}
\end{equation}
with non-inferiority margin $\delta = 0.03$. Training cost amortizes: if training
spent $C_{\mathrm{train}}$ tokens and the per-question saving is
$c_0 - c_T$ tokens, the \emph{break-even point}
\begin{equation}
N^{*} \;=\; \frac{C_{\mathrm{train}}}{c_0 - c_T}
\label{eq:breakeven}
\end{equation}
is the query volume after which curation has paid for itself---the single number
that tells a practitioner whether training a store is worth it for their workload.

The correspondence to supervised learning is exact: the store configuration is the
parameter vector, one training iteration is one optimization step, the training agent
is the optimizer, and held-out reader performance is the loss. Formally, the
environment with a \emph{fixed} reader policy induces a Markov process over store
configurations; training is a guided walk through configuration space, and the
questions are its supervision signal. Because training-agent forward passes and test
readers use identical tools, budgets, and the single-shot answer rule, the train/test
generalization gap is well defined: the three-layer decomposition compares accuracy on
re-encountered train instances (retention carried \emph{only} by the store---the
agent's memory resets every question), unseen instances of trained templates
(class-level generalization), and reserved templates (within-category transfer).

\section{The \kbgym{} Environment}
\subsection{Universe and Store}
A generator in the PhantomWiki style samples a fictional population (500 people in
the main configuration): family trees, spouses, friendships, per-person attributes
(job, hobby, city), globally distinct birthdates, and \emph{name distractors}---extra
people sharing first names with questioned subjects. Facts render through fixed
templates into $\sim$5{,}900 atomic single-sentence documents (``Alice Johnson's job is
arborist.''). The initial store has \emph{zero links}: a bag of atomic facts.
Document embeddings back \texttt{search} and are maintained
automatically as nodes are added, edited, or deleted.

\subsection{Actions}\label{sec:actions}
Reader and training-forward mode: \texttt{search(query)} (top-$k$ document ids
with full text), \texttt{read(id)} (text plus links, each with target text),
\texttt{answer(text)}. Training consolidation adds: \texttt{add(text)},
\texttt{edit(id,text)}, \texttt{delete(id)}, \texttt{link(a,b)},
\texttt{unlink(a,b)}, \texttt{done()}. Provenance is tracked throughout: initial
documents carry an origin; edited ones are flagged; authored ones have no origin.
Coverage and duplication are integer-exact; any authored content is attributable.

\subsection{Question Classes and Splits}
Ten categories over $\sim$26 templates. \emph{Forward} classes QC1--QC3 (1--3-hop
named-entity chains) are natively easy for document-level retrieval and serve as the
non-inferiority floor. \emph{Hard} classes target relations \emph{between}
documents: aggregation counts (QC4, QC8), abstention (QC5), multi-constraint joins
without a name anchor (QC6), set intersection (QC7), comparison/superlatives over
birthdates (QC9), and reverse lookup with a uniqueness guarantee (QC10). Golds are
computed by graph traversal over the fact layer; every question carries template id,
gold variants, and its support set. Splits (instance-disjoint): \texttt{train} (150;
i.i.d.\ over all categories, 60\% hard), \texttt{test\_in} (100; unseen instances of
trained templates), \texttt{test\_out} (50; one reserved template per category),
\texttt{eval} (30; stratified, drives the per-epoch learning curve so the final test
is touched exactly once). Grading is SQuAD-normalized F1/EM.

\subsection{Design Rationale}
Four decisions carry the methodology. \emph{(i) No LLM judge anywhere}: golds are
exact, grading is SQuAD normalization, store metrics are integer counts over
provenance---every number in this paper is reproducible to the digit.
\emph{(ii) Realistic retrieval}: \texttt{search} returns matched document text, as
production vector stores do; an early variant returning only titles collapsed the
reader to F1 $0.0$ and would have credited the trained store for fixing an
artificially crippled interface. \emph{(iii) Atomic documents}: the store is its
own chunking, so organization is expressible \emph{only} through links and navigation
nodes---any efficiency gain is attributable to structure, and any authored content is
provenance-flagged. \emph{(iv) Contamination control}: we measured gpt-5-mini
answering HotpotQA-style questions at F1 $\approx 1.0$ with \emph{no retrieval at
all}; on public corpora, a reader's score conflates the store with the weights, and
``store value'' loses meaning. A fictional universe makes the no-store score
$\approx 0$, so every point of reader performance is earned through the store.

\section{Training Protocol}
\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Training a knowledge base (reader and editor tool sets as defined
in Sec.~\ref{sec:actions})}
\label{alg:train}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State evaluate reader on \texttt{eval} \Comment{epoch-0 baseline (= B1)}
\For{epoch $= 1 \dots E$}
  \For{$(q, a^{*})$ in shuffled \texttt{train}}
    \State reset short-term memory; task $\gets q$
    \For{step $= 1 \dots N_1$}  \Comment{forward}
      \State $d \gets$ policy(reader tools)
      \If{$d = \texttt{answer}(\hat a)$}
        \State reveal $a^{*}$, F1$(\hat a, a^{*})$; \textbf{break}
      \EndIf
    \EndFor
    \For{step $= 1 \dots N_2$}  \Comment{backward}
      \State $d \gets$ policy(editor tools)
      \If{$d = \texttt{done}$} \textbf{break} \EndIf
    \EndFor
    \State refresh dirty embeddings; log
  \EndFor
  \State snapshot; evaluate reader on \texttt{eval} \Comment{curve point}
\EndFor
\State exam: frozen store, test splits, budget $M$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Phase 1 uses exactly the reader's toolset and budget, so train-forward accuracy is
directly comparable to test accuracy. The single-shot \texttt{answer} reveals the gold
in its result---standard supervised learning: predict, then see the label, then
update. Leftover Phase-1 budget is forfeited (no smuggling forward steps into
consolidation); a budget-exhausted forward scores $0$ but still receives the gold, so
failed questions---where repair matters most---get targeted consolidation.
Every intermediate store state is reconstructible by replaying the edit trace
(validated byte-exact against epoch snapshots), giving per-iteration store
trajectories for analysis at no storage cost.

The training agent's objectives, stated as goals (mechanisms are its choice):
(1)~answer correctly; (2)~consolidate verified reasoning into structure; (3)~organize
for the question's \emph{class}---build or extend index/navigation nodes so unseen
instances of the class resolve in fewer steps; (4)~keep the store
\emph{parsimonious}: duplicated facts compete in search and bury each other; delete
redundancy you create; if the store already serves the class, change nothing.
Objective~(4) was added after the negative result of
Section~\ref{sec:prelim}. Credit assignment needs no oracle: a wrong answer plus the
revealed gold lets the agent re-search \emph{with the answer in hand}, and the repair
is determined by the trajectory difference.

\textbf{\kbgym{} (main).} 500 fictional people (10\% name distractors; family
trees, spouses, friendships; job/hobby/city attributes; globally distinct
birthdates) render into 5{,}864 atomic documents. Ten question categories over
26 templates: forward chains QC1--QC3; aggregation counts QC4/QC8 (children,
friends, grandchildren, attribute-filtered counts); abstention QC5;
multi-constraint joins QC6 (no name anchor; unique answer enforced against
distractors); set intersection QC7 (common friends); comparison/superlatives
QC9 (birthdates; ties excluded by construction); reverse lookup QC10 (exactly
one qualifier). Golds are graph-derived with accepted variants; every question
carries its support set. Splits are instance-disjoint: \texttt{train} 150
(i.i.d.\ over all categories, 60\% hard), \texttt{test\_in} 100 (unseen
instances of trained templates), \texttt{test\_out} 50 (one reserved template
per category), \texttt{eval} 30 (stratified; drives the learning curve so the
final test is touched exactly once).

\textbf{Official PhantomWiki (external validity).} A generator we do not
control (phantom-wiki 1.0.3, SWI-Prolog 10.0.2): 16 family trees, 405 person
articles, 480 generated QA over 8 templates grouped into four composition
shapes, with per-question hop depth 1--5; two reserved templates (relational
and aggregative) form \texttt{test\_out}. Their Prolog-derived relations have
no per-fact supports, so repair diagnostics are unavailable on this arm; the
train/test protocol itself does not need them. Documents at sentence or
article granularity (the latter tests the method at paragraph scale with zero
contamination).

\textbf{MuSiQue (real-text arm)~\cite{musique}.} Paragraphs are the documents; the official
composition shapes are the categories, a held-out shape is
\texttt{test\_out}, and the annotated supporting paragraphs restore support
diagnostics. Real corpora are parametrically contaminated (we measured
near-ceiling no-retrieval scores for related datasets), so all scores on this
arm are reported as deltas over a mandatory no-store reader baseline.

\subsection{Baselines}
All baselines differ \emph{only} in how the store is prepared; reader, test
sets, and budgets are identical, and each reports its one-time build cost.
\textbf{B1, flat store}: the untrained store (= epoch~0). \textbf{B3,
GraphRAG-style}~\cite{graphrag}: entities extracted per document (lexical
extraction, justified by template-shaped text), co-occurrence communities,
one LLM-written community summary document per community linked to its
members. \textbf{B4, RAPTOR-style}~\cite{raptor} (extended version):
recursive embedding clustering with LLM summary documents per cluster,
hierarchically linked. \textbf{B5, HippoRAG-2-style}~\cite{hipporag2}:
(subject, relation, object) triples per document, entity-sharing links
between documents, per-entity hub documents. B3--B5 structure lands as
authored documents and links \emph{in our store format}, so the common
budgeted reader can traverse it: we test their \emph{structure} under a fixed
reader rather than their retrieval algorithms (a documented adaptation; e.g.\
B5 forgoes Personalized PageRank).

\subsection{Implementation}
Trainer and reader are gpt-5-mini (temperature 0.3, 4{,}096 tokens/turn),
tool-forced decisions, static schemas. Budgets $N_1{=}N_2{=}M{=}15$; FIFO
$K{=}30$; \texttt{search} returns top-5. Embeddings: chroma default ONNX
MiniLM family, environment-maintained. Measured training cost:
$\approx$23k tokens and 83\,s per iteration on the 120-person universe,
$\approx$161\,s on the 500-person main configuration ($\approx$\$6 per
300-iteration run at current pricing). Per-epoch snapshots plus byte-exact
trace replay reconstruct every intermediate store state for analysis
(merge events replay deterministically without the judge). Basic version:
single seed (seed~0); seed replication planned. The main run reported below
predates the dedup guard and uses prompt-level parsimony only; the guard is
default-on for subsequent runs, and its on/off contrast is a natural
ablation.

\subsection{Experiment Suite and Predictions}
\textbf{E1 (headline).} Learning curve: eval-split F1 and steps after each
epoch (epoch~0 = B1); final \texttt{test\_in}/\texttt{test\_out} pass on the
last snapshot, at $M \in \{8, 15\}$. Prediction: non-inferior accuracy with
fewer steps, and a leftward shift of the accuracy--budget curve (the trained
store dominates at tight budgets). \textbf{E2.} The baseline table: F1,
steps/question, build tokens, per-question tokens, for B1/B3/B4/B5/ours on
both test splits. \textbf{E3} (log slicing). Three-layer generalization
(train-forward from epoch~2 / \texttt{test\_in} / \texttt{test\_out}) and the
per-category profile. Prediction: forward classes QC1--QC3 are saturated near
1.0 at $M{=}15$ (they are the non-inferiority floor, not the headroom);
gains concentrate in aggregation (QC4/QC8), joins (QC6), intersection (QC7)
and reverse lookup (QC10), whose answers live in relations between documents;
QC5 abstention is expected unchanged. \textbf{E5} (trace mining). (i)
edit-type mix per epoch; (ii) store-size trajectory from replay (the
parsimony objective predicts sublinear growth, unlike the 5$\times$ blowup of
Sec.~\ref{sec:pollution}); (iii) morphology of authored navigation documents
(out-degree distribution, what they gather); (iv) structure--semantics
alignment: the share of built links connecting documents that co-occur in
some support set (supports are invisible to the agent, making the measure
honest); (v) mechanism attribution: correlation between a reader's step
savings and its hits on authored navigation documents and trained links.
Break-even $N^{*}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:breakeven}) closes the analysis.

\section{Preliminary Results}\label{sec:prelim}
\emph{Obtained on an earlier document-store variant of the environment (coarse multi-sentence
documents, copy/move actions) with gpt-5-mini, 120-person universe, 150 train
questions $\times$ 2 epochs; to be re-established on the final document-graph
environment.}

\subsection{A Pre-Organized Store Leaves No Headroom}
With one clean document per person, the untrained reader already scores 0.82
(\texttt{test\_in}) / 0.96 (\texttt{test\_out}): per-entity documents plus vector
search \emph{are} the optimal organization for entity-centric questions. Headroom for
accuracy requires genuine disorganization---our final design initializes at the
atomic-document level---while \emph{cost} headroom persists even at accuracy
ceilings, which is one reason efficiency is our primary endpoint.

\subsection{Additive Curation Pollutes}\label{sec:pollution}
Table~\ref{tab:pollution} shows the entity-initialized condition. Training an
already-organized store \emph{regressed} the reader at both budgets. The mechanism is
integer-exact in the provenance ledger: over 300 iterations the agent grew the store
from 120 to 593 documents, duplicated 381 of 1{,}184 facts, added 1{,}430 links---and
never invoked \texttt{move} or \texttt{delete}. Near-duplicate pages competed in
retrieval and buried each other; readers exhausted budgets wandering (27 question-level
regressions vs.\ 3 improvements). Curation with a consolidation objective but no
parsimony objective is monotone pollution.

\subsection{Calibration and Cost Profile}
Reader difficulty was calibrated by probes on untrained stores: forward named-entity
chains are natively easy for document-level retrieval at any tested scale (F1
$1.0$); headroom concentrates in the relation-level classes and grows with universe
size (untrained F1 $0.83$ at 120 people vs.\ $0.67$ at 500, mean steps $6.7$ vs.\
$9.2$)---motivating the 500-person main configuration, where the cost axis has
roughly $3\times$ headroom over the $\sim$3-step floor. Training costs
$\approx 23$k tokens per iteration (gpt-5-mini); a 300-iteration run is
$\approx \$6$ at current pricing.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\textbf{Curation is a boundary, not a slogan.} Section~\ref{sec:pollution} suggests
curation creates value where organization is lacking and pollution where it is not,
and the difference is carried by whether the optimizer knows when \emph{not} to edit.
The train/test protocol makes this boundary measurable; downstream-only evaluation
cannot see a store being slowly ruined while individual answers still look fine.

\textbf{Deployment reading.} The abstraction targets a real configuration: agent
fleets maintaining shared repositories, wikis, and knowledge bases. Our failure
ledger maps directly onto production incidents---duplicated near-copies burying each
other is the lost-update problem; authored-without-provenance content is the
hallucinated fix; a deleted last instance is knowledge loss during refactoring. The
break-even point $N^{*}$ answers the deployment question in units operators use:
after how many queries does curation pay for itself. The negative result doubles as a
default-behavior audit: an agent given a maintenance objective but no parsimony
objective degraded the asset it was tending while every individual action looked
constructive---a caution for unsupervised agent operation on shared state.

\textbf{Threats to validity.} Template-rendered language is simpler than natural
prose; phrasing regularity may flatter lexical matching (mitigated by embedding
retrieval and by the planned official-PhantomWiki arm, whose generator we do not
control). The reader and trainer share a model family, so structure tuned by the
trainer may suit that family's search habits. Single seed in the basic version;
single-agent by design (concurrent maintenance, with its conflict taxonomy, is the
natural sequel); support-set diagnostics are unavailable on the external arm.
Grading is token-F1 against short golds---adequate for entity answers, blunt for
free-form ones; the environment avoids the latter by construction.
